Why can’t we chat instantly?

Radio signals travel at the speed of light. That is very fast, but space is huge. The signal still needs time to cross the gap.

Each planet follows its own orbit around the Sun. Their different speeds make the gap grow and shrink. A smaller gap alone does not tell us when to launch a rocket.

For curious explorers: data and limits

These are 25 monthly snapshots from NASA/JPL Horizons, from January 2026 to January 2028. They are saved data, not live tracking.

We measure the straight-line distance between the centres of Earth and Mars in three dimensions, then divide by the speed of light. The diagram is a top-down projection. Dates are midnight TDB, the time scale used by this calculation.

This is an approximate radio-delay lesson. It does not calculate a rocket journey, the moving receiver’s exact light time, solar interference, signal blockage or relay delays. The shorter/longer buttons compare only these saved monthly samples.
